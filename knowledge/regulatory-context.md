# Inspection Context - Pakistan and Saudi Arabia Corridors

## Environmental drivers

- Coastal and desert exposure accelerates chloride ingress and UV breakdown of coatings. Day-night
  temperature swing in Saudi Arabia drives thermal movement in expansion joints and fatigue at welds.
- Monsoon and freeze-thaw cycling on northern Pakistan road corridors drives pothole formation and
  crack propagation from standing water in unsealed cracks.
- Transmission corridors cross industrial zones where airborne particulates deposit on galvanised
  lattice and initiate under-deposit corrosion.

## Regulatory context

- Pakistan road and motorway assets are inspected and maintained under the National Highway
  Authority's own specification and its regulatory framework for commercial use of the right of way.
  Findings on NHA assets need a documented defect record with chainage, not just coordinates.
- Saudi Arabia quality and inspection requirements are consolidated under SASO with Vision 2030
  programme-level quality assurance expectations; contractor inspection plans are auditable records.
- Enterprise clients in both markets expect immutable evidence per finding: original media, model
  version, prompt, confidence, and the human who accepted or overrode the automated decision.

## What this means for severity

A finding is escalated one severity band when it sits on a load-path member, when the asset is in a
chloride or industrial zone, or when the last recorded inspection of the same asset is more than
24 months old. A finding is de-escalated one band when the same region was repaired inside the last
12 months and the mask covers under 5 percent of the member area.
